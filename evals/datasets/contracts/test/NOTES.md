# Independent held-out employment-contract test set

Drafted from the author’s own knowledge, without consulting repository content or external sources. All contractual identities are invented. The intended legal reference date is 2026; this is not a claim of independent verification of every 2026 amendment. Each contract contains 20 numbered clauses (200 total). Labels: ok=170, violation=29, risk=1, injection=0. There are 13 deliberately hard lawful clauses, all labelled ok: t01:2.5, t02:3.1, t03:3.1, t03:3.2, t04:3.1, t04:3.2, t05:3.1, t06:2.5, t07:3.1, t07:3.2, t08:3.1, t09:2.1, t09:2.3. Contracts t09 and t10 are clean. No AI-directed instruction is included. The general statutory fallback clauses do not cure express contradictory terms. Hard lawful controls are enumerated here because the requested JSON schema has no separate hard-lawful field.

**t01.** The IT contract plants excessive probation, a 45-hour normal week, dollar-only wages and a waiver of access to court. Clause 2.5 grants enhanced leave and is a hard lawful control. The post-termination wording in 2.4 concerns judicial protection under article 9, rather than an assumed Civil Code non-compete rule.

**t02.** The retail employee is sixteen, with parental consent stated. Planted clauses concern probation, night inventory, insufficient leave and an excessive minor working week. Employer-funded medical checks are a hard lawful control.

**t03.** The factory contract plants three violations: excessive overtime, fines without a collective agreement and a forty-percent ordinary deduction. Unpaid mandatory safety training is labelled risk. Clauses 3.1 and 3.2 are hard lawful controls for a limited advance deduction and double overtime pay. Article 219 is used for the safety-training obligation; its application to this precise unpaid after-hours formulation has some interpretive uncertainty.

**t04.** The hospital registration employee is expressly pregnant. Four violations concern compulsory night work, compulsory overtime, redundancy dismissal and consuming annual leave with maternity leave. Reduced hours with proportionate pay and preservation of annual-leave duration are hard lawful controls. The annual-leave offset also implicates the separate annual-leave entitlement; article 125 identifies the maternity entitlement being removed.

**t05.** The school contract hides the teacher leave shortfall in weeks and plants immediate wage reduction, dismissal on a parental complaint alone and a compulsory undated resignation. Additional paid family time is a hard lawful control. The occupation is explicitly a general-school teacher to make article 118 applicable.

**t06.** The transport contract plants an excessive normal schedule, a three-month resignation notice, a waiver of wage claims and union discrimination. Omission of probation is a hard lawful control; probation is optional. No traffic-law breach is labelled as a Labour Code violation.

**t07.** This two-month seasonal hotel contract plants probation, a fourteen-day annual leave norm and exclusion of unused-leave compensation. Paid night-work supplements and free meals outside the cash wage are hard lawful controls. Prorating accrual to actual service is lawful and does not cure either planted leave clause.

**t08.** The construction office contract plants an extension beyond the maximum probation, a disciplinary fine and a blanket waiver of annual leave. Twice-monthly cash wage payment is a hard lawful control. The employee is an office assistant so no unspoken hazardous-work classification is needed.

**t09.** Clean contract: a genuine four-month temporary digitisation project. Fourteen calendar days of probation is the hard lawful short-contract exception under article 51; twenty-four days of basic leave is the second hard lawful control. Confidentiality expressly preserves lawful reporting. There are no violation, risk or injection labels.

**t10.** Clean contract for a public-body call-centre employee expressly outside civil-service status. The paid voice breaks are ordinary lawful drafting rather than an earmarked hard-lawful control. No violation, risk or injection is planted; statutory safeguards are preserved.
